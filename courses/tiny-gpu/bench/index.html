Build a tiny GPU

Which pixels does a triangle cover?

A GPU draws a picture as triangles. For each pixel it tests the pixel's centre against the triangle's three edges, and it colours the pixels whose centres fall inside. Every grid on this page is drawn by that test, the same JavaScript the course's learners write in lesson 1.

1. Two triangles, one edge

A square of 8 by 8 pixels is cut along its diagonal into two triangles, blue below and orange above. The diagonal runs exactly through the centres of 8 pixels. For each edge the test gives a number, w, positive on the triangle's side and 0 on the edge itself. For those 8 centres w is 0, so the test can't say which side they are on.

2. Drag a corner, count the warps

The course's GPU works out pixel colours, or shades them, a 4 by 2 block at a time. Each block is one warp of 8 lanes, one lane per pixel, all running the same instructions. Real GPUs use wider warps, 32 or 64 lanes. A lane whose pixel the triangle misses does no work, but the warp still carries it. The faint lines mark the blocks.

  • a pixel the triangle covers: its lane does work
  • a lane that waits
  • one warp, a 4 by 2 block

The big triangle keeps most of its lanes busy. A thin sliver, one pixel wide, crosses the grid. How busy will its lanes be?

Drag a corner, or pick one with the buttons and press an arrow key. Each press moves it 1/16 of a pixel, the grid the course snaps corners to. Hold Shift to move a whole pixel.
