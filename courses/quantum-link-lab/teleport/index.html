What does the pair check measure?

The Bell score uses 4,096 separate simulated calibration trials (seed 7). A score above 2 is the expected signature here. Those pairs were consumed by the check. Each teleportation trial uses a fresh simulated pair. Its delivery also depends on Bob acting on Alice’s message.

What do the corrections and detector do?

The X correction swaps 0 and 1. The Z correction flips their relative phase, which swaps + and −. Alice’s first message bit requests Z, and her second requests X. This bench prepares known test states. It checks 0 and 1 in the Z basis, + and − in the X basis, and +i and −i in the Y basis. A basis is a choice of what to measure. Each test state has a predictable answer in its own basis. The verifier knows the chosen test, while Bob’s receiver only uses the two message bits to decide which corrections to apply.
