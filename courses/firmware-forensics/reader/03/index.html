A matching hash answers one question

Compare a digest you computed with a digest someone declared. Agreement says the bytes fit that declaration. If the same person can replace both the payload and its digest, agreement survives the replacement. A second copy of the same declaration is not a second independent authority.

For example, a release may change diagnostics from minimal to verbose and update all its digest fields consistently. Internal integrity matches. If local policy requires minimal diagnostics, policy fails. Those observations can both be true. The frozen hash-only stage therefore always calls origin unverified.

A manifest can also be absent. “Not checked” is different from “checked and passed”. Preserve absence in the report. Review lab/lessons/01-evidence-and-change.md before implementing summarize_trust; the four outputs deliberately prevent one green boolean from erasing disagreement.

Change the requirement without changing the bytes

Local policy is a rule chosen for this exercise: required component names, required config values and a minimum release number. A release floor is the smallest release admitted by that rule. It is not a property written into the physical device.

A worked release numbered 4 meets floor 4 and fails floor 5. Its payloads, hashes and manifest can stay identical through that experiment. Copy the policy into your attempt, predict the outcome, then run. Keep both outputs to show that you changed the requirement rather than repaired the evidence.

In unit 04 a signature adds a verifiable relationship to a chosen key. It does not eliminate local policy or make a partial capture complete. Each new kind of evidence gets its own scope.
