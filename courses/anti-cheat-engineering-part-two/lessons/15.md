# 15 · Latency on the courier game

The courier arena is part one's game (README.md, "The two games"). A player steers a courier around a floor in 250 mm steps, picks up a parcel at the core and delivers it to the depot, each with a press of interact within 250 mm of the place, and every delivery scores a point. In part one the courier server and its client shared one machine, so a press reached the server the moment it was made. Here the courier arena is played over a network. A player's screen always shows the past, and a press always arrives late. By the end of this lesson your `courier/rewind.mjs` will judge each interaction at the moment the player saw, no further back than a window (a limit in ticks that you choose) and than that player's measured round trip. You will measure, for each window, how many honest presses are refused and how many lies are accepted, and choose a window against a budget.

The technique is usually called lag compensation. Multiplayer shooters use it to judge a shot against where the target was on the shooter's screen. The server keeps a short history of positions and rewinds to the shooter's moment, up to a limit the server sets. Valve, the game developer, describes one such system in "Source Multiplayer Networking", an article about its Source engine.

## A press, late

`public/courier.mjs` runs part one's rules: the courier moves 250 mm a tick while an admitted input is active (on each of the next 4 ticks), and an interaction needs it within 250 mm of its target, the core without a parcel or the depot with one. After an interaction counts, a cooldown makes the next one wait 4 ticks.

The boundary at `k × 50` ms starts tick `k`. At that instant the server admits the inputs that have arrived, runs tick `k` (the courier moves, and a press admitted at that boundary is decided) and sends the view of tick `k`. A view reaches the client one one-way latency later, and a press takes another to come back. The server admits a press at the first boundary at or after its arrival. Its move or stop takes effect in the tick that starts there, before the courier moves, and the press is decided after that move, at the position the ring holds for that tick. So a server judging "now" judges a press about one round trip after the moment the player saw.

The core, where the courier picks up a parcel, is a single point. Walking along the floor, the courier is within 250 mm of it at exactly three positions: one step before, on it, and one step past. At 250 mm a tick, it crosses that zone in three ticks, 150 ms.

## The position ring

To judge a press at an earlier tick, the server must remember where the courier was. `public/courier.mjs` keeps a ring of the last 40 ticks, where slot `tick % size` holds `{ tick, x, y }` for the newest tick written there, with `size` 40, so writing tick 40 overwrites tick 0. Read the size from `history.size`, as API.md says, not from this lesson. Forty ticks is two seconds, longer than any window this lesson will use.

Write `positionAt(history, tick)`: the position at the end of `tick`, or `null` when the slot holds a different tick or nothing. Check `15.position-at` reads rings after overwrites and asks for ticks the ring no longer holds. A ring is bounded retention, as part one's unit M9 taught it, meaning the server keeps what its rule needs and no more.

### Read a tick back from the ring

Tour `public/courier.mjs` first. `createHistory` makes the ring, `{ size, slots }`, with every slot null, and `recordPosition` writes `{ tick, x, y }` into slot `tick % size`, replacing whatever was there. Then find `step` inside `createCourierServer`: each tick it moves the courier, writes the new position into the ring, and only then decides a pending press, so the tick being decided is always in the ring when your judge runs. In `courier/rewind.mjs`, replace the `position-at` region:

<!-- code-example {"file":"examples/rewind-checkpoint.mjs","region":"position-at","language":"js","title":"A slot answers only for the tick it holds","change":"Replace the complete position-at region."} -->
```js {"file":"examples/rewind-checkpoint.mjs","region":"position-at","title":"A slot answers only for the tick it holds","change":"Replace the complete position-at region."}
export function positionAt(history, tick) {
  if (!Number.isInteger(tick) || tick < 0) return null;
  const slot = history.slots[tick % history.size];
  return slot && slot.tick === tick ? { x: slot.x, y: slot.y } : null;
}
```
<!-- /code-example -->

The slot for a tick is found by arithmetic, but a slot doesn't know which of the many ticks that share it was written last, so the stored `tick` is compared with the one asked for. A slot that holds another tick, or nothing, answers null instead of a position from another moment. A tick that isn't a whole number, or is negative, has no slot at all, and it is refused before the arithmetic, because `-1 % 40` is `-1` in JavaScript and `2.5 % 40` is `2.5`, neither of them a slot. The answer is a new `{ x, y }`, so a caller that changes it can't change the ring.

## Measuring the round trip

The input says which tick the player saw: `seen`. A server that rewinds to `seen` trusts the client's word about its own lateness. A client that wants to interact from out of range can claim to have seen an earlier tick, when the courier was in range.

The server can't see when a client looked at its screen, but it can time a round trip itself. Every 10 ticks it sends a ping, the client answers with a pong, and the server keeps the last 20 round trips in milliseconds. A client can't answer a ping before the ping reaches it, as long as it can't guess the ping, which is why real servers put an unguessable number in each one. (This course's server numbers its pings by tick, and none of its clients exploits that.) So a measured round trip is never shorter than the real one. It can be longer, because a client can hold its pongs.

Write `measureRtt(samples)`: the largest of the last 20 samples, in ticks (milliseconds divided by 50), or 0 with no samples. The largest is the generous choice, because an honest player whose connection hiccups keeps the rewind its worst recent trip needs.

Check `15.measure-rtt` gives your function lists of every length, including more than 20 samples.

### Keep the worst recent trip

Tour `receivePong` in `public/courier.mjs`: a pong for a ping the server is still waiting on becomes one sample, the time it arrived less the time the ping left, and the oldest sample is dropped once there are more than `RTT_SAMPLES`. The server passes your function a copy of that list, oldest first. Replace the `measure-rtt` region:

<!-- code-example {"file":"examples/rewind-checkpoint.mjs","region":"measure-rtt","language":"js","title":"The largest of the recent samples, in ticks","change":"Replace the complete measure-rtt region."} -->
```js {"file":"examples/rewind-checkpoint.mjs","region":"measure-rtt","title":"The largest of the recent samples, in ticks","change":"Replace the complete measure-rtt region."}
export function measureRtt(samples) {
  const recent = samples.slice(-RTT_SAMPLES);
  return recent.length ? Math.max(...recent) / TICK_MS : 0;
}
```
<!-- /code-example -->

`slice(-RTT_SAMPLES)` keeps the newest samples whatever the list's length, so a slow trip that has dropped out of them no longer counts, and a list shorter than that is kept whole. Dividing by `TICK_MS` gives ticks with the fraction kept: a 210 ms trip is 4.2 ticks, and the judge rounds it up where it needs whole ticks. An empty list gives zero, not `-Infinity`, which is what `Math.max()` of nothing would be. The probe comes in three parts, and the lesson asks for each one at the point where you have what it shows. ## The judge

`now` is the tick being decided: the tick that starts at the boundary where the press was admitted. Write `judgeInteract(args)` in the order API.md gives: refuse a `seen` in the future, then refuse when the cooldown hasn't run out (judged at now, because the cooldown is the server's own clock), then rewind to `max(seen, now - allowed)` with `allowed = min(window, ceil(rtt) + 1)`, and accept when the courier stood within range there. The extra tick covers a press that lands between two ticks.

A claim older than the rewind allows is judged at the oldest tick allowed rather than refused, which is what an honest player with a bad moment needs.

### Judge at the moment the client may claim

Tour `interact` in `public/courier.mjs`: it is the only place the server decides a press, and it builds your judge's arguments from its own state: `seen` from the input that asked to interact, `now` the tick, `rtt` your `measureRtt` of its samples, `target` the core or the depot, and `readyAt` the cooldown's first free tick. Whatever your judge returns, the server accepts the press only when `accept` is `true`. Replace the `judge-interact` region:

<!-- code-example {"file":"examples/rewind-checkpoint.mjs","region":"judge-interact","language":"js","title":"Three refusals, then a rewind bounded twice","change":"Replace the complete judge-interact region."} -->
```js {"file":"examples/rewind-checkpoint.mjs","region":"judge-interact","title":"Three refusals, then a rewind bounded twice","change":"Replace the complete judge-interact region."}
export function judgeInteract(args) {
  const { seen, now, window, rtt, history, target, range, readyAt } = args;
  const read = args.positionAt ?? positionAt;
  if (seen > now) return { accept: false, at: now, reason: 'future' };
  if (now < readyAt) return { accept: false, at: now, reason: 'cooldown' };
  const allowed = Math.min(window, Math.ceil(rtt) + 1);
  let at = Math.max(seen, now - allowed);
  let spot = read(history, at);
  if (!spot) {
    at = now;
    spot = read(history, now);
  }
  if (!spot) return { accept: false, at, reason: 'range' };
  const dx = spot.x - target.x;
  const dy = spot.y - target.y;
  const inRange = dx * dx + dy * dy <= range * range;
  return { accept: inRange, at, reason: inRange ? 'ok' : 'range' };
}
```
<!-- /code-example -->

The two refusals that don't look at the ring come first, and both are judged at `now`. A `seen` after `now` is a client claiming to have drawn a tick the server hasn't run. The cooldown belongs to the server's clock, so rewinding it would let a late press slip in under a cooldown that has already been running. Then the rewind: `Math.ceil` turns the round trip into whole ticks, the extra tick covers a press that lands between two ticks, and `Math.min` with the window means a client's round trip can only shrink the rewind, never stretch it past the limit you set. `Math.max(seen, ...)` means the judge never looks earlier than the tick the client says it saw, and a claim older than the bound is judged at the bound, not refused. If the ring has lost that tick, the press is judged at `now`, which the server always writes first. The range test compares squared distances, so it needs no square root, and a distance exactly at the range counts. `args.positionAt` is the reader the server was given, and the file's own `positionAt` stands in when a caller passes none.

## The window table

`runCourier` in `public/courier.mjs` plays sessions with three kinds of client:

- honest couriers on connections from 10 to 200 ms each way with up to 40 ms of jitter
- a naive faker on a 20 ms connection that answers pings honestly and claims to have seen up to 12 ticks earlier whenever that would put it in range
- a slow-pong faker on a 20 ms connection that holds its pongs, so its measured round trip looks like 300 to 400 ms, and claims to have seen accordingly

Each interaction comes back with whether it was accepted and two facts from the server's full record: was the courier in range at the tick the client claimed (`inRangeAtSeen`) and at the tick of the decision (`inRangeNow`). Write `tally(outcomes)` to turn them into one row: honest presses refused although the courier was in range at `seen` (legitimate rejections), and fakers' presses accepted although the courier was out of range at the decision (accepted cheats).

### Count one row of the table

Replace the `tally` region:

<!-- code-example {"file":"examples/rewind-checkpoint.mjs","region":"tally","language":"js","title":"Two kinds of error, counted per client","change":"Replace the complete tally region."} -->
```js {"file":"examples/rewind-checkpoint.mjs","region":"tally","title":"Two kinds of error, counted per client","change":"Replace the complete tally region."}
export function tally(outcomes) {
  const row = {
    honest: 0,
    legit: 0,
    legitRate: 0,
    naive: 0,
    naiveCheats: 0,
    slow: 0,
    slowCheats: 0,
  };
  for (const o of outcomes) {
    const cheat = o.accepted && !o.inRangeNow;
    if (o.who === 'honest') {
      row.honest += 1;
      if (!o.accepted && o.inRangeAtSeen) row.legit += 1;
    } else if (o.who === 'naiveFaker') {
      row.naive += 1;
      if (cheat) row.naiveCheats += 1;
    } else if (o.who === 'slowPongFaker') {
      row.slow += 1;
      if (cheat) row.slowCheats += 1;
    }
  }
  row.legitRate = row.honest ? row.legit / row.honest : 0;
  return row;
}
```
<!-- /code-example -->

Each outcome counts once, under its client. The two errors are judged by different facts, on purpose. An honest press is a legitimate rejection when it was refused although the courier was in range at the tick the player saw, because that is the moment the player acted on. A faker's press is a cheat when it was accepted although the courier was out of range at the tick of the decision, because the server gave it something its courier wasn't standing close enough to earn. The rate is over all honest presses, and it is zero, not `NaN`, when there are none. Check `15.tally` gives your `tally` crafted outcomes, and check `15.sweep` runs the courier with your functions on its own seeds and compares every decision with the server's record.

### Pick the window that lets the fewest lies through

Replace the `pick-window` region:

<!-- code-example {"file":"examples/rewind-checkpoint.mjs","region":"pick-window","language":"js","title":"Within the budget, the fewest slow-pong cheats","change":"Replace the complete pick-window region."} -->
```js {"file":"examples/rewind-checkpoint.mjs","region":"pick-window","title":"Within the budget, the fewest slow-pong cheats","change":"Replace the complete pick-window region."}
export function pickWindow(rows, budget) {
  let best = null;
  for (const row of rows) {
    if (row.legitRate > budget) continue;
    const fewer = !best || row.slowCheats < best.slowCheats;
    const tie = best && row.slowCheats === best.slowCheats;
    if (fewer || (tie && row.window < best.window)) best = row;
  }
  return best ? best.window : null;
}
```
<!-- /code-example -->

A row over the budget is skipped, whatever it costs the faker. Among the rest, a row replaces the best so far when it lets fewer slow-pong cheats through, or as many from a smaller window, so a tie goes to the smaller window and the answer doesn't depend on the order of the rows. When no row is within the budget there is nothing to choose, and the answer is null rather than the least bad window. Only the slow-pong column decides, because the naive faker is already bounded by its own round trip. Check `15.pick` gives your `pickWindow` crafted rows, including ties and a budget nothing meets.
