# 17 · The scoreboard

Five lessons built five defences, each measured on its own. A defence is judged as a whole. A strict admission that refuses an honest player's message, or a detector tuned so tightly that it flags experts, fails as surely as one that lets a cheat through. This lesson runs everything at once.

By the end of it you will have chosen your courier window, run the scoreboard, and met every budget below on sessions you have never seen.

## Start

This lesson builds on your unit 16 project: keep working in the same folder. Run every command from the course's folder, the one that holds `lab.mjs`.

**What is new.** Almost no code. Everything the scoreboard plays is yours already: your admission and recorder from lesson 12, your view from lesson 13, your frozen detector from lesson 14, your judge from lesson 15 and your trade from lesson 16. The one new file is `capstone/defence.json`, which holds the window you chose in lesson 15 and your sentence on why. This lesson is about reading the whole defence's numbers and judging them.

**Before this unit** `capstone/defence.json` is the starter's, so `node lab.mjs scoreboard project` stops before it plays anything, with `capstone/defence.json: not written yet`.

**After this unit** the scoreboard plays your whole defence and marks each of nine budgets PASS or MISS, and you can follow one of its rows, the courier, from the window in your file to the line it prints.

**Code scope.** No code for you to write in the walkthrough. One supplied file in `examples/`, `examples/courier-row.mjs`, 89 lines, reads the courier row with the scoreboard's own functions, and the lesson shows its three marked regions, 53 lines. Two supplied files are toured with it: five parts of `checks/scoreboard.mjs`, a 583-line file (`windowOf`, `loadRewind`, `courierAt`, `courierVerdict` and `BUDGET`), and two of `labcmds/17.mjs`, a 248-line file (`SEEDS` and `DEFAULTS`, and the lines of `scoreboard` that print the courier).

**What stays yours.** Every setting the scoreboard reads is yours on every route: the window and its reason in `capstone/defence.json`, and the detector and threshold you froze in lesson 14. No route writes either, and the lesson shows no window or threshold to copy.

**Time.** No learner has been timed on this lesson yet.

## The scoreboard

```
node lab.mjs scoreboard project
```

It uses every file you wrote, so a function still unwritten stops it, whichever row needs it. It plays, with your admission and your view on the Lob server and your frozen detector over every session:

- each legal player model, on its own connection
- each protocol cheat from lesson 12
- the searching client, live against your view, and once more against the full view, to show what hiding bought
- the adapted searchers' recordings, through your detector

Then your judge at your window on the courier arena, and your trade under a crash after every write.

It takes from 10 to 90 seconds, depending on the machine. On the course's worked solution, the Lob table reads:

```

  skilled                    500  10.0% (597/6,000)          0      0        0
  expert                     500  25.9% (1,553/6,000)        0      0        3
  highLatency                500  8.6% (519/6,000)           0      0        0
```

`refused` counts sessions in which your admission refused an input for a reason other than `stale`, and `stale` counts sessions with a stale refusal. Read it row by row.

- The legal players: none refused, one jitter session with a message overtaken on the network and refused as stale, which is allowed, and 3 of 3,000 sessions flagged, all experts'.
- The protocol cheats: every session refused. `hitClaim` never lands a shot, because every one of its fires carries the forbidden field. `replay` is refused only as stale, because a byte-for-byte replay is an old seq.
- The searching client: 64.8% against your view, 100.0% against the full view, and every session flagged.
- The recordings: the same 22 and 0 of 60 as in lesson 14.

Under the table come the courier at your window (the worked solution's window is 7: 24 of 20,444 honest presses refused, no naive lie accepted, and the slow-pong faker's presses accepted 41,660 times out of 44,696) and the trades (no item held twice or by nobody, and no coin made or lost, over 93 crash points). Then each budget, PASS or MISS.

## Read one row: the courier

A number on the scoreboard is only as useful as your understanding of where it comes from. Follow one row, the courier, from your file to the lines it prints. `examples/courier-row.mjs` does what `node lab.mjs scoreboard` does for that row, with the same functions, seeds and counts, and prints each step.

### Read the window

Tour `labcmds/17.mjs` first: `SEEDS` and `DEFAULTS` near its top are the scoreboard's seeds and sessions, and in `scoreboard` find the lines that load your rewind functions, read `capstone/defence.json` and call `courierAt`. In `examples/courier-row.mjs`, the first region does the same reading:

<!-- code-example {"file":"examples/courier-row.mjs","region":"read-window","language":"js","title":"The window, read as the scoreboard reads it","change":"Study the supplied region."} -->
```js {"file":"examples/courier-row.mjs","region":"read-window","title":"The window, read as the scoreboard reads it","change":"Study the supplied region."}
const loader = makeLoader(project);
let window;
if (given === undefined) {
  let defence;
  try {
    defence = loader.json(sb.FILES.defence);
  } catch (error) {
    stop(error);
  }
  const read = sb.windowOf(defence);
  if (read.problem) {
    console.log(read.problem);
    process.exit(1);
  }
  window = read.window;
  console.log(`${sb.FILES.defence}: rewindWindow ${window}`);
} else {
  window = Number(given);
  if (!Number.isSafeInteger(window) || window < 0)
    stop(
      new Error(`WINDOW is a whole number of ticks, 0 or more, not ${given}`),
    );
  console.log(
    `window ${window}, given on the command line (${sb.FILES.defence} is not read)`,
  );
}
```
<!-- /code-example -->

`makeLoader` is the loader the checks use, and `loader.json` reads your file from the project; a file still holding the starter's words stops the tour with the same one-line sentence the scoreboard prints. `windowOf` in `checks/scoreboard.mjs` accepts only a JSON object whose `rewindWindow` is a whole number of ticks, zero or more; anything else, a window still `null` included, comes back as a `problem` sentence, which is what stops the scoreboard before it plays. Given a window on the command line, the tour uses that one instead, so you can compare.

### Play the courier at that window

```js {"file":"examples/courier-row.mjs","region":"play-courier","title":"Your judge at your window, on the scoreboard's sessions","change":"Study the supplied region."}
const rewind = await sb.loadRewind(loader).catch(stop);
```

<!-- /code-example -->

Tour `loadRewind` and `courierAt` in `checks/scoreboard.mjs`. `loadRewind` loads your `positionAt`, `measureRtt` and `judgeInteract` and wraps each so that an error names your function and the call. `courierAt` runs `runCourier` from lesson 15 at the window with your functions, then counts its outcomes by lesson 15's rules: an honest press refused although the courier was in range at `seen`, and a faker's press accepted while it was out of range now. These are your `tally`'s rules, applied by the course's own code so that a mistake in your `tally` can't move a budget.

### Judge the budgets

<!-- code-example {"file":"examples/courier-row.mjs","region":"verdict","language":"js","title":"Two rates against two budgets","change":"Study the supplied region."} -->
```js {"file":"examples/courier-row.mjs","region":"verdict","title":"Two rates against two budgets","change":"Study the supplied region."}
const verdict = sb.courierVerdict(courier);
for (const [what, part, budget] of [
  ['honest presses refused', verdict.honest, sb.BUDGET.honestRefused],
  ["the naive faker's lies accepted", verdict.naive, sb.BUDGET.naiveAccepted],
])
  console.log(
    `  ${part.pass ? 'PASS' : 'MISS'}  ${what}: ${percent(part.rate)}, at most ${percent(budget)} allowed`,

```
<!-- /code-example -->

`courierVerdict` turns the counts into two rates, each over its own client's presses, and compares each with its entry in `BUDGET` at the top of `checks/scoreboard.mjs`: at most, so a rate exactly at the budget passes. ## The budgets

The checks run the same scoreboard on fresh seeds: `17.protocol` the protocol cheats, `17.legal` the legal players, `17.searcher` the searching client, `17.courier` your window and `17.trade` your trade. They require:

| Row | Budget |
| --- | --- |
| each protocol cheat | every cheating input refused, in every session |
| legal players, all six models | no input refused except as `stale` (a message the network delivered after a newer one); at most 1 session in 125 flagged (12 of the 1,500 sessions the check plays) |
| expert players | hit rate at least 18% |
| the searching client, live against your view | hit rate at most 75%; at least 90% of its sessions flagged |
| courier, at your window | honest presses refused at most 1%; the naive faker's lies accepted at most 1% |
| trades | every item held by exactly one player and no coin made or lost, after a crash at any write |

Question 17a: your view adds a field `windExact` holding the server's exact wind. Which budget fails? (a) legal players: no input refused (b) the searching client's hit-rate ceiling (c) the courier (d) trades

```
node lab.mjs predict project 17a YOUR_ANSWER
```

Each budget belongs to a lesson. The expert floor fails if your admission refuses legal messages or your view drops something the screen needs. The searching client's ceiling fails if your view leaks something it reads, and question 17a's feedback says what that is. A leak under a name or encoding it doesn't read doesn't help it, so the ceiling doesn't catch that leak, but unit 13's checks do. Its flag floor and the legal flag ceiling pull against each other. That's your detector's threshold, and the budget you set it for in lesson 14.

## What the scoreboard doesn't show

- Every legal player is a model, not a person. A detector that flags none of them has been shown to flag none of them.
- No budget limits the slow-pong faker, because no window can. At the window that refuses no honest press, it gets nearly every claim through. The scoreboard prints what it gains, so the cost of your choice is in front of you.
- The scoreboard has no cheat the course didn't write. The searching client aims, but it doesn't, for example, play two accounts, or share what one player sees with another. Each of those needs its own measurement.

## Use the idea on a new case

Suppose the team that runs the courier arena reads that last point and asks for a budget on the slow-pong faker after all: not on its lies, which no window can stop, but on what they buy it. The faker may deliver at most 20% more parcels a session than an honest courier.

## Your own engine (optional)

If you took Sightline, your engine plays Lob through a host, the interface Sightline gives your engine to a copy of Lob on your machine: `binary()`, `memory()`, `press()`, `tick()`, `screen()`.
