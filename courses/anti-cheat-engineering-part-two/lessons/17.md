# 17 · The scoreboard

Five lessons built five defences, each measured on its own. A defence is judged as a whole. A strict admission that refuses an honest player's message, or a detector tuned so tightly that it flags experts, fails as surely as one that lets a cheat through. This lesson runs everything at once.

By the end of it you will have chosen your courier window, run the scoreboard, and met every budget below on sessions you have never seen.

## Your window

Lesson 15's table priced every window in honest presses refused against lies accepted. Write the window you chose, a whole number of ticks, into `capstone/defence.json` in place of the starter's `null`, and the sentence on why in `why`. The checks read only the window, and the sentence is for whoever reads your project:

```json
{ "rewindWindow": null, "why": null }
```

The scoreboard's budget for honest presses refused is 1%, judged on fresh sessions. A window that met the budget by a hair on lesson 15's sessions can miss it on new ones, so leave a margin.

## The scoreboard

```
node lab.mjs scoreboard project
```

It uses every file you wrote, so a function still unwritten stops it, whichever row needs it. It plays, with your admission and your view on the Lob server and your frozen detector over every session:

- each legal player model, on its own connection
- each protocol cheat from lesson 12
- the searching client, live against your view, and once more against the full view, to show what hiding bought
- the adapted searchers' recordings, through your detector

Then your judge at your window on the courier arena, and your trade under a crash after every write.

It takes about 15 seconds. On the course's worked solution, the Lob table reads:

```

  skilled                    500  10.0% (597/6,000)          0      0        0
  expert                     500  25.9% (1,553/6,000)        0      0        3
  highLatency                500  8.6% (519/6,000)           0      0        0

  recording delay             60  68.1% (490/720)            -      -       22
  recording delay-misses      60  31.1% (224/720)            -      -        0
```

`refused` counts sessions in which your admission refused an input for a reason other than `stale`, and `stale` counts sessions with a stale refusal. Read it row by row.

- The legal players: none refused, one jitter session with a message overtaken on the network and refused as stale, which is allowed, and 3 of 3,000 sessions flagged, all experts'.
- The protocol cheats: every session refused. `hitClaim` never lands a shot, because every one of its fires carries the forbidden field. `replay` is refused only as stale, because a byte-for-byte replay is an old seq.
- The searching client: 64.8% against your view, 100.0% against the full view, and every session flagged.
- The recordings: the same 22 and 0 of 60 as in lesson 14.

Under the table come the courier at your window (the worked solution's window is 7: 24 of 20,444 honest presses refused, no naive lie accepted, and the slow-pong faker's presses accepted 41,660 times out of 44,696) and the trades (no item held twice or by nobody, and no coin made or lost, over 93 crash points). Then each budget, PASS or MISS.

## The budgets

The checks run the same scoreboard on fresh seeds: `17.protocol` the protocol cheats, `17.legal` the legal players, `17.searcher` the searching client, `17.courier` your window and `17.trade` your trade. They require:

| Row | Budget |
| --- | --- |
| each protocol cheat | every cheating input refused, in every session |
| legal players, all six models | no input refused except as `stale` (a message the network delivered after a newer one); at most 1 session in 125 flagged (12 of the 1,500 sessions the check plays) |
| expert players | hit rate at least 18% |
| the searching client, live against your view | hit rate at most 75%; at least 90% of its sessions flagged |
| courier, at your window | honest presses refused at most 1%; the naive faker's lies accepted at most 1% |
| trades | every item held by exactly one player and no coin made or lost, after a crash at any write |

Question 17a: your view adds a field `windExact` holding the server's exact wind. Which budget fails? (a) legal players: no input refused (b) the searching client's hit-rate ceiling (c) the courier (d) trades

```
node lab.mjs predict project 17a YOUR_ANSWER
```

Each budget belongs to a lesson. The expert floor fails if your admission refuses legal messages or your view drops something the screen needs. The searching client's ceiling fails if your view sends the exact wind as a top-level `windExact`, the name the full view gives it (`public/full-view.mjs`), because the searching client reads that field from any view. It uses the full view's other hidden values only when a message carries all five numbers it needs under `game`: `windExact`, `flagSpeed`, `flagTimer`, `rng` and `targetX`, as the full view does. A leak under any other name or encoding doesn't help it, so the ceiling doesn't catch that leak, but unit 13's checks do. Its flag floor and the legal flag ceiling pull against each other. That's your detector's threshold, and the budget you set it for in lesson 14.

## What the scoreboard doesn't show

- Every legal player is a model, not a person. A detector that flags none of them has been shown to flag none of them.
- No budget limits the slow-pong faker, because no window can. At the window that refuses no honest press, it gets nearly every claim through. The scoreboard prints what it gains, so the cost of your choice is in front of you.
- The scoreboard has no cheat the course didn't write. The searching client aims, but it doesn't, for example, play two accounts, or share what one player sees with another. Each of those needs its own measurement.

## Your own engine (optional)

If you took Sightline, your engine plays Lob through a host, the interface Sightline gives your engine to a copy of Lob on your machine: `binary()`, `memory()`, `press()`, `tick()`, `screen()`. Against this server it gets none of those, only messages. `public/adversaries.mjs` shows how the searching client turns messages into a local copy of the game, and an adapter that gives your engine such a copy is a small file. This isn't checked, and the scoreboard doesn't run it unless you add it.
