# 06 · Compare complete expeditions

A director changes the states it will see next. Compare complete episodes under matched conditions before deciding whether to adopt a candidate.

## Pair the experiment, allow paths to diverge

An **episode** is one expedition, from initial state to defeat or the twelve-round finish. A snapshot test asks what a policy would choose in one saved state. It cannot reveal the later consequences of changing that choice. Run both policies from the same initial seed, bot and target profile, then let each trajectory evolve.

A **pair** contains one baseline episode and one candidate episode with the same case identity. The supplied engine indexes hazards by seed and round. Both policies therefore face the same hazard for round five even if their earlier choices consumed different numbers of random draws. Their health and legal actions may still differ because earlier choices differed.

The manifest freezes the list of episode IDs, seeds, player bots and profiles. Do not remove a case after a timeout or defeat. Those outcomes are part of the experiment. Development, calibration and evaluation each contain sixty episodes with disjoint seed families. These counts are bounded teaching datasets, not a statistical guarantee.

## Recompute the product outcomes

Your metrics function first audits the complete transition trail, then reads events and decision records. The auditor reconstructs the seed-driven world and compares the final state, including its terminal reason. A healthy prefix is missing evidence and must be rejected, even if its event and decision arrays have equal lengths. Report survival and completion separately. Survival means health remains above zero when the run ends. Completion additionally requires twelve resolved rounds and at least twelve charge. Challenge exposure is the number of encounter rounds divided by twelve. All-rest has survival one, completion zero and challenge zero.

For each resolved round, distance from the target interval is max(lower − pressure, 0, pressure − upper). Values inside the band have zero distance. Average over the fixed twelve-round denominator. If a defeat ends after four rounds, the eight unplayed rounds each contribute distance one. Also report actual-played-round distance separately. Otherwise a quick defeat could look excellent because it avoided difficult later rounds.

Keep proposal violations separate from executed violations. A rejected brute proposal that falls back to rest is still a proposal violation. Count fallback reasons, request reservations, latency and duplicate/stale effects. Nonzero fault controls check every count; an all-zero summary cannot pass. Fixture timing is simulated; do not call it measured network latency. The event record includes origin so a fixture is never silently merged with a live capture.

For completion practice, imagine two played rounds with distances 0.1 and 0.2 followed by defeat. The fixed-denominator distance is (0.1 + 0.2 + 10)/12. Compute it before running your metric. Then compare it with the played-only average and explain which denominator could hide failure.

## Summarize paired differences

For each pair, subtract baseline from candidate. Negative target-distance differences favor the candidate; positive completion differences favor the candidate. Keep the direction explicit rather than declaring every positive number good. Report every bot/profile slice with its sample count. An improved overall mean can coexist with unacceptable degradation in one slice.

The supplied resampler draws complete pairs with replacement and recomputes the mean. It does not draw individual rounds. An interval that crosses zero is compatible with either direction under the resampling procedure; it does not prove equality. The deterministic resampling seed makes the report reproducible, not more certain.

Implement compare in evaluate.mjs. Open Function contracts (API.md), section 06, for the required comparison report shape and exact fallback-counting labels. The runner needs per-pair slice identities and both complete metric objects to check your arithmetic. Test it first by pairing a policy with an identical copy: every difference should be zero. Then reorder the input pairs and check that the conclusion is unchanged. Reject mismatched seed, bot, profile and episode identity. An average over unrelated cases loses the benefit of pairing.

## Freeze an adoption rule

Before final evaluation, record hard gates: no executed violations, no duplicate or stale effects, no request allowance exceeded, and complete event accounting. Then state acceptable completion and survival degradation and the minimum target-distance improvement worth adopting. Use development results to choose these limits. Changing them after seeing final results requires a new experiment identity.

The CLI's fixture evaluation runs the complete selective controller, including calibration and fallback, with the same request limit you froze. A zero allowance makes zero requests. The runner independently recomputes every report field before applying your recommendation. In the shipped synthetic corpus, weak confidence evidence may make rules the defensible choice. A rule-based release is a valid outcome. Keep the raw episodes and a report explaining the decision.

Your fresh problem is a candidate that improves the mean but harms the cautious/pulse slice. Use the published degradation limit, not enthusiasm for the overall chart. Automated checks validate arithmetic and consistency. Your explanation of why those limits fit the product remains a reasoning task for the rubric and later review.
