# 01 · Read the game before choosing

Your expedition has health 6 after three rounds, but that number does not tell you whether damage is accelerating. Build an observation that describes what the director knows now, without exposing the future.

## State becomes an observation

The **state** is the game's complete record. The **observation** is the subset available to a decision policy. A **policy** maps an observation to an action. A **transition** applies an action and the player's move to produce the next state. The supplied engine owns transitions. You own observations and, later, the policy.

Start the demonstration below. Choose Press for one encounter and Brace for the next. Watch the health and charge columns in the round log. Press earns the card's full charge and takes its threat plus a hidden hazard of zero or one. Brace earns one less charge and prevents up to two damage. Repair consumes a supply, heals three before damage, and earns no charge. Rest has no player move: it restores two health and one supply. The twelve-round deadline means avoiding every encounter cannot light the beacon.

Before your next move, predict whether Brace will produce at least as much health as Press on the same card and hazard. Then try it with Replay same seed. Replay resets both the game and the director's memory, so the comparison begins from the same situation. The hazard is indexed by seed and round, so changing how many random numbers a policy requests cannot change the round's hazard.

## Measure recent pressure

**Pressure** is a designed pacing signal, not a measurement of fun. It combines missing health with damage in the last three resolved rounds. Divide missing health by twelve and weight it by 0.6. Divide recent damage by six, cap that fraction at one, and weight it by 0.4. Add the terms. The target is an interval: a steady expedition requests pressure between 0.25 and 0.55.

For health 6 and damage [2, 1, 0], the missing-health term is 0.6 × 6/12 = 0.3. The history term is 0.4 × 3/6 = 0.2. Pressure is 0.5. At the same health with [0, 0, 0], pressure is 0.3. A policy can distinguish recovering from recent damage from staying at the same health for several quiet rounds.

At round zero, three observations do not exist. Return pressure as null until there are three actual events. Null means unavailable; zero means measured and calm. Preserve the actual short recentDamage array and set historyComplete false. Also return null whenever observationMissing is true. A later policy may still use known health, but it must not invent a measurement.

## Implement the public boundary

The API reference defines the exact fields. Read the current state without modifying it. Select the last three resolved events, extract damage and player moves, compute pressure only when available, and copy the episode identity and revision. A **revision** identifies which state a later answer belongs to. It advances once per resolved round. Do not copy the complete state with object spread: that would also copy the seed and any future-only test metadata.

In the editor, complete observe.mjs. Start by returning the health and recentDamage fields. Run Check to see the remaining contract failures. Then add the explicit field list from API.md. The first incomplete run is saved; repairing it appends another attempt. Help declarations and predictions belong to each attempt, so record them before checking.

For a completion problem, use the worked calculation above but leave out the last division: health 9 and recent damage [1, 2, 0] contribute 0.15 from health. Compute the history term and finish pressure. Then solve a fresh case with health 8 and damage [0, 0, 0], without using the worked numbers.

## Debug a leak and transfer

Imagine an observation includes nextHazard. Two games have identical public histories but different next hazards. Their observations must remain identical. A difference would let the director use information unavailable when the player acts. This is a causal boundary, not merely a naming convention. The checker supplies extra hidden fields and changes them while holding the public prefix fixed.

After your first repair, use Fresh public variant. It checks a different deterministic prefix while keeping the requirement unchanged. These cases are visible and repeatable; they are practice, not protected assessment. Add your own pair of same-prefix states with different hidden fields and describe what should remain equal. Keep that example for module 08.

Before continuing, you should be able to explain why pressure can be null at health 12, why the seed is excluded, and why two health-6 states can carry different pressure. A passing checker supports the observation contract. It does not establish that pressure captures a human player's experience.
