# 02 · Make illegal choices impossible

A proposal can spell “brute” correctly and still be forbidden in the current state. Build the complete legal action set before choosing any encounter.

## Intersect the constraints

An **action** is the director's choice of rest, scout, swarm or brute. The player chooses Press, Brace or Repair only after an encounter appears. Keep these two decisions separate. The legal-action function accepts your observation and returns an array of allowed action names, with no duplicates.

Each encounter spends director budget: scout costs one, swarm two and brute three. The expedition starts with eighteen budget points. Rest costs zero and remains available in every nonterminal state. At health three or below, rest is the only action. Swarm also needs health at least six; brute needs at least eight. Swarm and brute are high encounters. A high encounter cannot immediately follow either high encounter. That one-round restriction is a **cooldown**.

An action must satisfy every applicable rule. Budget permission does not override low health. Health permission does not override cooldown. First handle terminal states: health zero or round twelve has no legal action, including rest. Then consider each action using the conjunction of its constraints. Conjunction means all conditions hold; disjunction means at least one holds.

For health 7, budget 2 and previous swarm, scout and rest remain legal. Brute fails its cost and health tests as well as cooldown. Swarm passes cost and health but fails cooldown. Checking only the first reason can be enough for control flow, but retain all reasons when explaining a rejected proposal.

## Complete a filter

Work through one candidate at a time before writing a compact filter. Start with scout at health 4 and budget 1. The state is not terminal, health is above three, and budget covers one. Scout has no additional health threshold and is not high, so the previous card does not affect it. Scout is legal.

Now keep health and budget fixed and replace scout with swarm. Fill the missing final check yourself: cost two cannot be covered by budget one. Do not keep swarm merely because health exceeds three. Next test health 8, budget 3 and previous scout. List the legal set without copying the worked answer; this case combines both high-action thresholds with a clear cooldown.

Implement allowedActions in constraints.mjs. A table of costs and minimum health is a reasonable approach. Separate predicates are also valid. The checker accepts either order of the returned set. It rejects duplicates, extra actions, missing actions and mutation of the observation. Returning only rest is safe in some states but incomplete whenever scout is also legal.

## The executor remains authoritative

Your policy will eventually propose an action. The supplied engine checks the original state again immediately before commitment. This catches a stale or defective policy, but a rejected proposal still counts as a proposal violation. The trace records both proposed and executed actions. It does not award your policy correctness because the engine recovered with rest.

This separation matters when a delayed response arrives. A brute proposal may have been legal at revision 4, then become illegal after another choice advances the game. Module 07 will bind the response to a revision. For now, distinguish choosing within a set from verifying that the set still belongs to the current state.

## Debug the tempting OR

A broken filter says “enough budget OR enough health.” At health 2 with budget 18 it admits a brute. That counterexample isolates the Boolean error: abundant budget cannot buy permission to ignore the recovery rule. Construct a second counterexample where health is abundant but budget is exhausted. Changing both inputs in one test without predicting each constraint makes diagnosis harder.

Your public check enumerates health 0 through 12, budget 0 through 18 and each possible previous card. Its oracle uses a separately written table of thresholds. The table and your implementation can still share a misunderstood specification, so keep your hand-worked boundary cases. Enumeration verifies this finite domain; it does not prove arbitrary extensions correct.

For transfer, use a terminal state with healthy health and unused budget. The temptation is to return rest because it is free. Explain why round twelve ends permission for every action. Then create a near-boundary pair at health five and six with budget two and previous rest. Only one condition changed. Predict exactly which action becomes legal, run your function, and preserve the result in your report.

Move on when the complete set passes and you can distinguish malformed action, illegal action and stale action. These are different failure reasons, even if all eventually lead to the same fallback.
