# Evidence rubric

Code and reasoning are separate judgments. Public checks exercise stated behavioral contracts. They do not prove learning, independent transfer or production readiness.

For each claim, score 0 (absent or contradicted), 1 (partially supported), or 2 (supported by reproducible evidence):

1. Observation and constraints: distinguish public history from hidden future; explain a boundary case and preserve executor rejection.
2. Baseline and tradeoff: show meaningful completion and exposure, and a case where recovery helps one metric at a cost to another.
3. Uncertainty: name the calibrated event, split by episode, report per-domain row and distinct-episode counts, intervals and risk/coverage; handle domain shift without pooling it away.
4. Comparison: retain complete paired episodes, adverse slices, failures, interval and frozen adoption limits.
5. Diagnosis: preserve a controlled failure, a single-cause intervention, negative control and remaining alternative.
6. Release decision: choose rules, selective use or more evidence consistently with the frozen policy; reproduce export and recovery.

Every claim used to adopt a candidate needs level 2. A total cannot compensate for false provenance or a violated hard gate. A supported decision to retain rules passes the reasoning goal.

Strong example: “Our synthetic controller abstained on all eligible bins because their lower bounds missed the frozen threshold. Candidate outcomes matched rules while consuming four reservations per episode. We retain rules for this environment; this says nothing about a real provider.”

Weak example: “The model is safer because no player died.” This omits charge, exposure, pairing, origin and uncertainty. If the evidence is synthetic, calling it a model result is false.

Self-review is immediately available. Another assessor can review the same artifact without a service gate. Independent held-out transfer and human timing/learning remain unverified until corresponding trials occur.
