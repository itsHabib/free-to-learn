# 08 · Capstone: level 3

Level 3 is the one level no lesson shows, and you finish it with the toolkit you built and nothing else.

Before this page your toolkit has proved one level, broken another and steered a bell. After it, you have a level 3 movie the untouched game replays, and an account of how it works in the form a speedrun site publishes next to a run.

**Before you start:** modules 01 to 07 pass. The capstone adds no code to `work/tas.mjs`, and `node lab.mjs route 08` says so and changes nothing.

**Time:** about 3 minutes to read. Building has no fixed length: a finishing movie may take an hour, and the top tier much longer.

## The level

The level file is `game/levels/level3.txt`. Open it and read it as you read the others: `#` is stone, `<` a belt, `^` spikes, `B` the bell, `F` the flag, `S` the start. Its header has two lines the bell room also had: `seed:` is the random state at frame 0, and `drop: block 13 6,9,12,15` says the bell drops a block in row 13, at column 6, 9, 12 or 15, with rows and columns counted from 0 at the top left. Open it in the player with `node lab.mjs view --level level3 ./work`, where `--level` opens a level with no movie, record a few frames, and save them: the movie's header names level 3.

![Level 3 drawn from its level file, 50 by 16 tiles, par 200. Kettle starts at the right, under the bell. A belt running left leads to a spike pit that crosses the runway. The bell's four drop places sit over the pit in row 13, at columns 6, 9, 12, 15. The flag is at the far left, behind a wall one tile thick. No route is drawn.](figures/level3-map.svg)

The flag sits behind a wall one tile thick. The spike pit is 14 tiles wide, columns 4 to 17. The only way over it that keeps Kettle's speed is a block from the bell, and the bell chooses where the block lands from the random state, as in module 7.

## The tiers

This command replays `work/movies/level3.movie` on the untouched game and names the tier it reaches:

```
node lab.mjs capstone ./work
```

The tiers:

- **Finish:** Kettle reaches the flag, in any number of frames.
- **Beat par:** the flag within 200 frames.
- **Match the best count:** the flag no later than the author's best count. The command prints that count once your movie beats par, and `checks/expected.json` holds it for anyone who looks. It isn't proven optimal. The author's exact search of level 3 was stopped at its limit of 8 million states, at depth 72, without an answer, for the reason module 7 gave: until the bell rings the random state stays in the key, and states that differ only there never merge.

No check rejects a movie for being too short. If you beat the author's count, keep your `work/movies/level3.movie`: it is shorter than any movie the author found, and the capstone command prints its frame count.

## How to approach it

One search with the random state in the key did not finish level 3, so break the level into **segments**, short stretches each with its own goal, and join them with savestates.

- If you use A*, write your own estimate. Module 5's `walkingBound` measures the distance to a flag on Kettle's right; level 3's flag is on its left, so it returns 0 there and A* searches as breadth-first search does.
- Steer first. Decide which drops bridge the pit, then find a start that rings the bell for one of them, by idle frames or by a search with `withRandom` and a goal on the drop byte (0x14). Try more than one drop before you settle on one.
- Search each segment from the savestate where the last one ended, with a goal you write as a test on the RAM: past a column, at a speed, standing on a tile. Lesson 06 gives the speed the clip needs, more than 4 px a frame, and its watch shows where the clip happened on level 2.
- Check each joined movie with `node lab.mjs play level3 work/movies/level3.movie`. A segment that works alone can fail after the join if its savestate was taken from a different movie.
- Minimise the glitch segments with `minimise` before you explain them. Your account then has to explain only the presses that remain.

A search that keeps a whole state object per node, as yours does, used about 450 to 550 MB for about 600,000 states (lesson 06), and the random state in the key keeps apart states that would otherwise merge. Set a `budget`, run one search at a time, and narrow the goal when a search stops on its budget.

## What to hand in

- `work/movies/level3.movie`, the movie the capstone command judges.
- `work/notes/level3.md`, a short account: what each segment does, which drop you steered and how, and the addresses that show each glitch, with the frames where they appear (x at 0x02, vx at 0x06, the drop at 0x14 and so on). Nothing checks the notes: they are for you, and for anyone who wants to replay your movie and understand it.

Then run:

```
node lab.mjs capstone ./work
```

## Stretch

Both tasks are optional, and neither has an automated check yet.

**The item-slot write.** The vault (`game/levels/vault.txt`, par 60) has four gems on the floor and a bell above the start whose drop is a gem. Kettle holds up to four gems in the slots at 0x0e to 0x11, and the next byte, 0x12, is the level-complete flag. Read `touch` in `game/core.mjs`. The game decides once per frame whether there is room, before it looks at the gems Kettle touches. When Kettle holds three gems and touches two in one frame, both pass that test, and the game writes the second into the fifth slot, which is the level-complete byte. Walking right collects four gems and reaches the flag in 189 frames. Steer the bell's gem so that the fifth write happens, and the vault completes within par with no flag at all. Judge it with:

```
node lab.mjs play vault work/movies/vault.movie
```

**Patch it and prove it.** Copy `game/core.mjs` to `work/patched-core.mjs`, never editing the original, and change the push-out rule so that it always pushes Kettle back the way it came. Copy your toolkit to `work/patched-tas.mjs` and change its first line to import from `./patched-core.mjs`. Then show the clip is gone: your 70-frame level 2 movie no longer finishes on the patched game, and a search of the clip yard from `game/fixtures.mjs` for any state past its wall comes back exhausted, where the untouched game crosses it in 49 frames.
